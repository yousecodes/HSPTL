# HSPTL — Full-Letter Monogram Exploration

**Design brief:** Explore distinctive logo constructions in which all five letters — **H, S, P, T and L** — are integrated into one compact HSPTL mark. The goal is not to place a standalone H icon beside the word HSPTL. Each concept below makes the complete five-letter name the source of the symbol.

**Visual direction:** Midnight Cobalt  
**Brand context:** A modern digital teaching hospital; hospital-inspired destinations remain part of the product identity.  
**Status:** Written concept exploration only. These are design directions and construction briefs, not finished vector artwork or proof of small-size legibility.

---

## 1. Shared-Spine Monogram

**Idea:** Use one strong vertical architectural spine as the shared structure for several letters. H, P and L share a left-hand stem; T contributes a top crossbar; S becomes a curved or angled path through the centre.

**Construction brief**
- Start with a vertical central/left spine.
- Build the H crossbar and P bowl from the upper portion of the spine.
- Extend a top beam to imply T.
- Extend the lower stroke to form L.
- Weave an angular S through the open middle space without obscuring the other letter cues.
- Use consistent stroke widths and rounded or clipped terminals, not a mix of styles.

**Text schematic (structural idea, not a literal drawing):**

```text
H ─┐
│  ├── T
├──┘   ) S
│  P   └── L
│
```

**Personality:** Architectural, engineered, institutional.  
**Strength:** The letters can share real strokes, making the result compact rather than five unrelated glyphs.  
**Risk:** If the S is too abstract, the mark may read as HPTL. Keep a recognisable S-turn in the centre.  
**Best use:** Primary brand symbol and application header.

---

## 2. Continuous-Ribbon HSPTL

**Idea:** Draw HSPTL as one continuous folded ribbon. The path starts as H, turns into S, forms the bowl of P, crosses into T, and finishes as L.

**Construction brief**
- Use a single continuous band with a consistent width.
- Make each letter transition into the next; avoid simply writing five letters in a connected script.
- Use geometric bends rather than calligraphic loops so the mark feels clinical and precise.
- A subtle two-tone split can distinguish the start and finish, but the logo must also work in one colour.

**Text schematic:**

```text
H──╮
│  ╰─S──╮
│      P╰──T──┐
└─────────────┘ L
```

**Personality:** Flow, continuity, learning progression.  
**Strength:** Strong metaphor for a learner moving through hospital departments and clinical stages.  
**Risk:** A connected five-letter ribbon can become difficult to decipher at 16 px. Keep this as a more expressive primary lockup, with a simplified small-size derivative.  
**Best use:** Website masthead, presentation covers, splash screen and larger brand placements.

---

## 3. Five-Module Hospital Grid

**Idea:** Represent each letter with one modular architectural element. The five elements interlock into a compact floor-plan-like symbol. The modules are not five separate tiles; they share edges and negative spaces to form one mark.

**Construction brief**
- Use a 5-unit design grid, with H, S, P, T and L each assigned a consistent module.
- Arrange the modules in a compact 2-by-3 footprint, leaving one cell open as a doorway/corridor.
- Share edges between adjacent letters to make the monogram feel like one building plan.
- Preserve a distinct silhouette when reduced; avoid fine internal gaps.

**Text schematic:**

```text
┌── H ──┬── S ──┐
│       │       │
├── P ──┼── T ──┤
│       │       │
└── L ──┴─ open ┘
```

**Personality:** Digital hospital architecture, modularity, navigation.  
**Strength:** Directly supports the hospital-directory interaction and department-based information architecture.  
**Risk:** Could resemble a generic app grid or medical cross. Avoid a symmetric plus-sign silhouette; use a distinctive perimeter and a clear L-shaped ending.  
**Best use:** Product icon, hospital directory trigger and navigation identity.

---

## 4. Negative-Space Portal

**Idea:** Build a solid architectural portal or shield-like outer form. Cut the five letters into it as negative space, so the letters are discovered inside the structure rather than drawn as separate strokes.

**Construction brief**
- Establish a simple outer arch/portal silhouette.
- Carve H, S, P, T and L into the interior through carefully shared counters and cut-outs.
- Keep the outside silhouette simple enough to recognise independently.
- Test the mark in a single solid colour; do not depend on gradients to reveal the letters.

**Text schematic:**

```text
╭───────────╮
│ H  S  P   │
│   T   L   │
╰───┐   ┌───╯
    └───┘
```

**Personality:** Premium, quiet, architectural, confident.  
**Strength:** A distinctive silhouette that can evoke entering a digital teaching hospital.  
**Risk:** Negative-space letterforms can collapse at small sizes. The five letters may be more legible in the full-size mark than in the app icon.  
**Best use:** Main brand symbol and larger signage-like placements.

---

## 5. Interlocking Typographic Monogram

**Idea:** Use custom-drawn versions of the five uppercase letters and interlock them through shared stems and crossbars. The result should still feel typographic: viewers should be able to identify HSPTL rather than decode an abstract picture.

**Construction brief**
- Design the five glyphs from the same geometric type system.
- Let the H stem become the P stem; let the T crossbar connect into the L; use a compact S as the linking centre.
- Use open counters and generous internal gaps.
- Keep the overall silhouette wider than it is tall, avoiding a dense square knot.

**Text schematic:**

```text
H┬S┬P
 │ │ └─┐
 └─T───L
```

**Personality:** Typographic, modern, confident and brand-led.  
**Strength:** The strongest route if the requirement is that people should actually see all five letters.  
**Risk:** Can look like a complicated wordmark rather than a symbol if the letter overlaps are too literal.  
**Best use:** Primary wordmark-symbol combination, app header and documentation.

---

## 6. Stacked Vertical Lettermark

**Idea:** Stack and compress the five letterforms into a tall architectural column, like a vertical wayfinding marker. Every letter remains a recognisable uppercase form, but the total shape becomes a single emblem.

**Construction brief**
- Arrange H, S, P, T and L along a shared vertical axis.
- Let adjacent letters share horizontal terminals and vertical stems.
- Keep the S as the visual hinge between the upper and lower halves.
- Make the outer silhouette resemble a narrow tower or hospital wayfinding sign.

**Text schematic:**

```text
┌── H ──┐
   S
┌── P ──┐
   T───
   L___
```

**Personality:** Monumental, structured, institutional.  
**Strength:** An unusual silhouette that can work well in a vertical sidebar or stacked brand placement.  
**Risk:** Less adaptable to wide horizontal placements; letter order must remain obvious.  
**Best use:** App splash screen, vertical signage, sidebar identity and badges.

---

## 7. The HSPTL Wordmark-as-Symbol

**Idea:** Do not force all five letters into a pictorial icon. Instead, create a custom, tightly kerned HSPTL wordmark where the five letters are modified to share architectural cuts, terminals and proportions. The word itself becomes the logo.

**Construction brief**
- Draw custom H, S, P, T and L glyphs on one grid.
- Use a shared vertical cut or doorway-shaped counter as a recurring motif.
- Let the crossbar of T and foot of L align with the other letter terminals.
- Keep the word recognisable at small sizes; use a separate neutral app tile containing the same custom wordmark when space permits.

**Text schematic:**

```text
H S P T L
│ │ │ │ └─ shared baseline / L-foot motif
└─┴─┴─┴── consistent architectural cuts
```

**Personality:** Minimal, typographic, contemporary and highly ownable.  
**Strength:** Maximum letter recognition; avoids the problem of a symbol that hides some characters.  
**Risk:** The mark is wider than a conventional app icon. Do not compress the wordmark into a tiny square and expect it to remain readable.  
**Best use:** Official logo, navigation header and institutional documents.

## Midnight Cobalt design system

Use the same palette across the seven directions so the logo construction—not colour—determines the choice.

| Token | Hex | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Midnight Navy | `#172B40` | Main logo, dark navigation and headings |
| Cobalt | `#285B87` | Secondary logo face, active navigation |
| Steel Blue | `#6D9FC5` | Secondary accents and data visualisation |
| Ice Blue | `#E6EFF5` | Light surfaces and page backgrounds |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Logo knockouts and cards |
| Gold | `#D6A34A` | Restrained highlight, milestones and non-critical emphasis |
| Clinical Red | `#C95D5D` | Clinical warning/error only; not a brand accent |

**Monochrome rule:** Every concept must work as pure one-colour navy on a light background and pure white on a dark background. The logo must not depend on gradients, shadows, gold accents or colour separation to make the letters distinguishable.

## Recommended design route

Develop **three finalists** rather than polishing all seven equally:

1. **Shared-Spine Monogram** — best balance between a compact symbol and recognisable letters.
2. **Five-Module Hospital Grid** — strongest connection to the hospital directory and modular product architecture.
3. **Interlocking Typographic Monogram** — safest choice when explicit recognition of all five letters is the highest priority.

Keep the **Wordmark-as-Symbol** as the official full-width wordmark even if one of the other three becomes the app icon. This creates a coherent identity without requiring a tiny icon to carry more detail than its size allows.

### Acceptance criteria before selecting a final logo

- All five letters H, S, P, T and L are intentionally represented in the full-size mark.
- Letter order is not ambiguous when the logo is explained to a first-time viewer.
- Works in pure monochrome: navy on white and white on midnight navy.
- No meaning depends on gradients, shadows or colour differences.
- At 32 px, the HSPTL identity remains recognisable.
- At 16 px, the mark retains a distinct silhouette and does not turn into visual noise. It is acceptable for the complete letters to be less individually readable at 16 px, provided the full wordmark is available nearby and the icon is not the only accessible name.
- Includes horizontal, stacked, app-icon, light-background and dark-background lockups.
- Uses clear-space, minimum-size and one-colour reproduction rules.
- Is redrawn as clean vector paths (SVG/PDF) before production use; these concept descriptions are not production artwork.

## Final recommendation

Start with **Shared-Spine**, **Five-Module Hospital Grid** and **Interlocking Typography**. They offer three genuinely different answers to the core challenge: shared letter anatomy, modular hospital architecture and explicit typographic recognition. Compare those three in monochrome before reintroducing the Midnight Cobalt palette.
